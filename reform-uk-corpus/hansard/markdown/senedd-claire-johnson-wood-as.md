## 2026-06-23 — 2. Business Statement and Announcement

*Speech* · [https://record.senedd.wales/Plenary/16076#C764458](https://record.senedd.wales/Plenary/16076#C764458)

Llywydd, I'd like to request a statement from the Cabinet Minister for education on additional learning needs funding levels in Wales. It is clear that the additional learning needs system in Wales is under strain, with councils' spending on ALN increasing by a staggering 34 per cent in real terms between 2018-19 and 2025-26. Not only that, but an Audit Wales report concluded that poor data collection and ineffectual public bodies are holding back the entire system. Children with ALN can't wait years and years for a fix. So, will the education Minister make a statement outlining what she will do to improve this failing system that we have? And what guarantees will she give that the Barnett consequentials coming to Wales for ALN are actually spent on ALN?

## 2026-07-07 — 2. Business Statement and Announcement

*Speech* · [https://record.senedd.wales/Plenary/16083#C766467](https://record.senedd.wales/Plenary/16083#C766467)

Trefnydd, every year, around 400 people in Wales are diagnosed with leukaemia. The outcomes can vary significantly depending on where they live, with many patients facing delays in diagnosis and unequal access to specialist treatment and care. Leukaemia UK's recent report, 'Turning ambition into progress for people with leukaemia in Wales', sets out practical recommendations to improve outcomes, including early diagnosis, equitable access to treatment, stronger cancer leadership and better data collection—all of this ensuring that leukaemia is fully reflected in future cancer policy. Leukaemia sufferers and their friends, family and loved ones in my constituency of Gwynedd Maldwyn have asked that the Minister for health and social care considers these recommendations and set out what action the Welsh Government will take to ensure that people affected by this disease no longer face a postcode lottery in accessing the care and support that they need.

## 2026-07-15 — 1. Questions to the Cabinet Minister for Health and Care / Cadriovascular Disease

*Speech* · [https://record.senedd.wales/Plenary/16086#C768035](https://record.senedd.wales/Plenary/16086#C768035)

Thank you, Minister. I have personal experience of this issue. My father was rushed into hospital in Hereford, across the border, 42 miles away from home, after suffering a heart attack whilst, ironically, teaching cardiopulmonary resuscitation. He had then to be transferred to Wolverhampton—and this was actually the hospital that he was born in—where he was eventually given a quadruple heart bypass. The journey there and the length of his stay, and the difficulty in visiting him, all added to the stress of the experience. The whole experience was stressful for him and his loved ones. The scale of the challenge posed by cardiovascular ill health can be judged by the fact that British Heart Foundation Cymru say that there are around 26,000 people living with cardiovascular disease in Gwynedd Maldwyn alone today, and that number is growing. This disease causes 57 deaths each month and 680 deaths each year in my constituency alone. The British Heart Foundation calculate that the total annual healthcare cost of this disease across Wales is £770 million. Whilst the National Adult Cardiac Surgery Audit includes data for waiting times and surgery performed in England and Wales, not all data is available for Wales, which makes it much harder to scrutinise the Wales NHS performance.
