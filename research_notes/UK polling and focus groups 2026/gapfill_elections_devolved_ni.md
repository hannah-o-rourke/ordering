## Q1. Scotland: pre-election Holyrood poll series, the 7 May 2026 result, post-election VI (May–Nov 2026) and independence polling in 2026

### Takeaway
The full Wikipedia series (159 constituency and 160 regional poll rows, 34 constituency polls in 2026) shows the SNP leading every 2026 constituency poll by 12–24 points with Labour and Reform fighting for second in the high teens; the result (SNP 38.2% constituency / 27.2% list, 58 seats; Labour 19.2/16.0, 17; Reform 15.8/16.7, 17; Greens 2.3/14.0, 15; Conservatives 11.8/11.8, 12; Lib Dems 11.4/9.4, 10; turnout 53.4%) sat inside the final polls' range, with Survation (constituency MAE 0.64) and YouGov (list RMSE 1.25, 63/73 constituencies called) the most accurate. Post-election polls (six to 23 Oct) keep the SNP at 32–38% constituency and 26–31% list with Labour 15–21% and Reform 13–19%; 30 independence polls in 2026 put Yes between 40% (YouGov) and 52% (Norstat/Stonehaven), a 50/50 race only when don't-knows are excluded.

### Inferences
- Every final poll had the SNP first by double digits and Labour/Reform within 3 points of each other, so the "who comes second" story was correctly framed; the main misses were on the list ballot (More in Common's Reform 22% and Greens 10% vs 16.7/14.0) and on seats, where six of eight MRPs/aggregators projected an SNP majority or near-majority (59–67) against the actual 58 — the constituency vote (38.2%) came in at the low end of the polls (35–41) and the Greens' two constituency gains plus four Lib Dem gains from the SNP cost it seats.
- Post-election polls show no "Burnham bounce" for Scottish Labour at Holyrood (15–21% constituency, below its 19.2% result in four of six polls) and a sharp Green fall-back on the constituency ballot (3–9%) while its list vote (7–19%) is volatile by pollster; Reform is steady at 13–19%.
- On independence, Swinney's 21 May claim that "polls show support for independence is now in the majority" holds only for don't-know-excluded figures (e.g. Norstat 52/43 → 55/45) and only for Norstat, Find Out Now and Stonehaven; YouGov (40–43% Yes) and Ipsos (42–46%) never showed a Yes lead in 2026. House effects of roughly 8–10 points between YouGov and Norstat dominate the series.

## Q2. Wales: 2026 Senedd poll series, result and post-election polling (including Westminster VI in Wales)

### Takeaway
Polls under the new 96-seat closed-list system (43 rows, 18 in 2026) moved from Plaid leads of up to 14 points in January to a near tie in April; the final polls (Plaid 30–35, Reform 28–29, Labour 9–16) bracketed the result (Plaid 35.4%, Reform 29.3%, Labour 11.1%, Conservatives 10.7%, Greens 6.7%, Lib Dems 4.5%; turnout 51.6%, the highest ever for a Welsh devolved election), and YouGov's final MRP got the seat count exactly right for Plaid (43), Reform (34), Greens (2) and Lib Dems (1). Three post-election polls (Aug–Sep) have Plaid 32–33, Reform 27–31 and Labour recovering to 13–18, with More in Common putting Labour at 22% for Westminster in Wales.

### Inferences
- Constituency polling in 2026 systematically understated the eventual winner's share: Greens by 8–20 in Gorton, Burnham by 6–12 in Makerfield (the generic-ballot Reform lead of 11 became a 20-point Labour win), Farage was overstated by 10 in Clacton (a boycott-and-novelty contest), and only in Holborn were the two polls within a point or two. ## Q6. Polling accuracy on 7 May 2026 and pollster post-mortems

### Takeaway
Reachable post-mortems are the pollsters' own: Survation claims the lowest Holyrood vote-share errors (constituency MAE 0.64, list 1.26) and closest SNP seat call (59 vs 58); YouGov claims the most accurate Holyrood MRP (63/73 constituencies, list RMSE 1.25, second on national seats with SNP +4 and Con −5) and "by some distance" the most accurate Senedd projection (seats exactly right for four parties, Labour 12% vs 11.1%); Electoral Calculus reports every party within its ranges in both nations but a central SNP 65 vs 58; the English locals saw Greens below their polls and Labour above the seat models (Wikipedia/Curtice), and Makerfield/Gorton constituency polls missed the winner's share by 6–20 points. No independent (BPC/academic) audit was reachable.

### Cited Findings
- Survation: "Six polling firms published final calls ... Survation's final call was the most accurate of any pollster"; constituency MAE 0.64, "the only MAE below one percentage point"; list MAE 1.26; MRP 59 SNP seats vs 58, "our overall seat projection error was the lowest of any firm producing an MRP model"; method: "a combination of online and telephone fieldwork", a Scotland-specific MRP "not treated as a regional subset of a GB-wide model"; polling partners Scotland in Union, University of Strathclyde, Diffley Partnership and Ballot Box Scotland — [Survation, 11 May 2026](https://www.survation.com/survation-was-the-most-accurate-pollster-at-the-2026-holyrood-elections-and-had-the-most-accurate-overall-scottish-mrp-seats-forecast/).
- YouGov (Patrick English, 12 May 2026): "most accurate in terms of individual constituency calls, getting the right winner in 63 out of Holyrood's 73 seats"; "most final MRP models ... managed to correctly project more than 60 constituency results"; constituency-level mean absolute error 2.4 vs 3.4 for the next competitor; "second most accurate" on national seat totals ("a four-seat overestimate on the SNP total, and a five-seat underestimation on the Conservative total"); list RMSE 1.25 vs 1.31 for the next competitor; constituency RMSE 1.20 with "a two-point underestimate of the Conservative share and a two-point overestimate of the Reform share"; used Chris Hanretty's notional 2021 results and the 2025 Hamilton, Larkhall and Stonehouse by-election to calibrate turnout — [YouGov](https://yougov.com/en-gb/articles/54738-how-yougov-accurately-called-the-2026-scottish-parliament-election). Wales: see Q2 — [YouGov](https://yougov.com/en-gb/articles/54735-yougovs-was-the-most-accurate-seat-projection-of-the-2026-welsh-senedd-election); both listed on [YouGov's election track record](https://yougov.com/en-gb/articles/54778-yougovs-election-track-record).
- Electoral Calculus: Scotland central forecast SNP 65 / Reform 21 / Labour 13 / Green 12 / Con 11 / LD 7, all actuals within ranges; Wales Plaid 35 (range 25–42; actual 43 "outperformed that range by one seat"), Reform 33, Labour 13, Con 9, Green 4, LD 2 — [EC Scotland](https://www.electoralcalculus.co.uk/blogs/ec_scot_20260506.html); [EC Wales](https://www.electoralcalculus.co.uk/blogs/ec_wales_20260506.html).

- English locals: "the Greens underperformed opinion polls while Labour overperformed prior seat projections" (Britain Elects and More in Common had projected 1,087–1,096 Green and 458–616 Labour councillors against 587 and 1,068) — [Wikipedia: 2026 UK local elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_Kingdom_local_elections). Labour move ahead by 4 points which is the first lead recorded by them since March 2025" — [UK Polling Report](https://pollingreport.uk/articles/the-burnham-bounce-has-arrived/).

### Inferences
- On the two devolved contests the industry performed well on vote shares (most final polls within 2 points on every party except Labour in Wales and the SNP in a couple of Scottish polls) but seat models over-read the SNP's constituency dominance; MRPs that projected an SNP majority (YouGov Mar–Apr, JL Partners, Stonehaven, EC/Find Out Now) all did so before the Greens' and Lib Dems' late constituency gains became visible.
- The consistent 2026 pattern — Greens polling above their eventual share (Holyrood list excepted), Labour above its seat projections, winners under-polled in by-elections — points to late tactical consolidation against Reform, which Electoral Calculus's tactical-voting work (July) later quantified as costing Reform 39 Westminster seats.
