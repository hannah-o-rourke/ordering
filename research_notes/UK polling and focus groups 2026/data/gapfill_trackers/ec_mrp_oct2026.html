MRP Poll October 2026

This page first posted 16 October 2026

On behalf of PLMR, Electoral Calculus has conducted a new MRP poll. Polling using MRP, which stands for Multi-level Regression and Post-stratification, is a relatively recent innovation in polling science which have been used to successfully predict the last three British general elections, and gives the estimated result in each Westminster constituency.

Fieldwork was conducted from 25 September - 2 October 2026, with a sample size of over 6,600 people.

More details at the PLMR website.

1. Headlines

The poll places Labour on 28pc, ahead of Reform on 21pc and the Conservatives on 18pc. On these figures, Labour is predicted to win 353 seats, giving it a comfortable majority of 56 seats. The Conservatives are set to be the official opposition with 90 seats, with Reform as the third-largest party with 81 seats.

The Liberal Democrats would win 70 seats, and the SNP 28 seats. Plaid Cymru would win 5 seats, and the Greens 4.

2. Tactical Voting

Following a major polling project on tactical voting (TV) by Electoral Calculus and Find Out Now, we are able to estimate the effect of tactical voting on the predicted election result. Our TV analysis shows that:

  • Strong anti-Reform tactical voting could cost Reform dozens of seats
  • Over a third of Labour voters say they would vote Conservative to keep Reform out
  • Otherwise voters on the left and right respectively support other candidates on the same side

3. Local Decisions

With devolution in the air, we asked our respondents "Who do you think should have the most say over decisions about local services, such as transport, housing and schools, in your area?"

Most people (52pc) said it should be their local council, with 25pc saying 'Don't know'. A further 12pc said it should be 'A regional mayor or combined authority', and only 7pc said it should be the UK Government.

In Wales, the situation is more balanced with 32pc favouring local councils, and 30pc choosing the Welsh government.

4. Treatment of "Don't Knows"

Following polling errors in 2024, Electoral Calculus took part in investigations into possible causes, co-ordinated by the British Polling Council. Our findings are available here. In summary,

  • Centre-left voters were more likely to over-estimate their likelihood to turnout
  • Those who didn't know or refused to answer might have voted according to their GE 2019 preference

To incorporate these findings into our post-election polling, we have made two assumptions about those who answer "don't know" to either the turnout or voting intention questions.

  • Those who say "don't know" to the turnout question are assumed to be unlikely to vote
  • Those who say "don't know" to the voting intention question are assumed to vote in the same way as GE 2024

Technical Details

Find Out Now polled 6,699 GB adults online between 25 September and 2 October June 2026. The sample was weighted to be representative by gender, age, social grade, other demographics and past voting patterns. Regression techniques were used to infer projected seat results.

Find Out Now and Electoral Calculus are both members of the British Polling Council and abide by its rules.

Regression Polling

Modern polling analysis often uses statistical regression techniques to get more accurate and geographically detailed results. Also called MRP (multi-level regression and post stratification) they have been used successfully by Electoral Calculus and other pollsters to predict general elections, local elections and the 2019 European elections.
