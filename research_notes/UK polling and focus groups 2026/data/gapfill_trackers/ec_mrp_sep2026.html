MRP Poll September 2026

24 September 2026

On behalf of Restore Britain, Electoral Calculus has conducted a new MRP poll. Polling using MRP, which stands for Multi-level Regression and Post-stratification, is a relatively recent innovation in polling science which have been used to successfully predict the last three British general elections, and gives the estimated result in each Westminster constituency.

Fieldwork was conducted from 4-11 September 2026, with a sample size of over 5,500 people.

1. Headlines

The poll places Labour in first place on 25pc, with Reform on 22pc, Conservatives on 20pc and Restore Britain on 7pc. On these figures, Labour is predicted to win 288 seats, leaving it 38 seats short of an overall majority, while the Conservatives are set to win 165 seats, Reform 84, and Restore 1 (Great Yarmouth).

The Liberal Democrats would win 61 seats, and the Greens 7 seats. The SNP would win 18 seats, and Plaid Cymru 7 seats.

2. Tactical Voting

Following a major polling project on tactical voting (TV) by Electoral Calculus and Find Out Now, we are able to estimate the effect of tactical voting on the predicted election result. Our TV analysis shows that:

  • Strong anti-Reform tactical voting could cost Reform dozens of seats
  • Over a third of Labour voters say they would vote Conservative to keep Reform out
  • Otherwise voters on the left and right respectively support other candidates on the same side

If a general election were held now, 40pc of Reform voters would be likely to vote for Restore Britain, including almost a quarter (23pc) who would be "very likely" to do so. That figure drops to 12pc among Conservative voters who said they would be likely to vote for Restore Britain. Labour and Liberal Democrat voters were lowest, at 7pc and 6pc respectively.

Across the electorate as a whole, 13pc of voters said they would be likely to vote for Restore Britain, against 58pc who said they would be unlikely to. But with almost a quarter (22pc) still unsure, the figures suggest Reform remains Restore Britain's main target base, with potential to attract those who are undecided.

Unsurprisingly, the majority of Labour voters (61pc) and Liberal Democrat voters (57pc) have an unfavourable opinion.

4. Treatment of "Don't Knows"

Following polling errors in 2024, Electoral Calculus took part in investigations into possible causes, co-ordinated by the British Polling Council. Our findings are available here. In summary,

  • Centre-left voters were more likely to over-estimate their likelihood to turnout
  • Those who didn't know or refused to answer might have voted according to their GE 2019 preference

To incorporate these findings into our post-election polling, we have made two assumptions about those who answer "don't know" to either the turnout or voting intention questions.

  • Those who say "don't know" to the turnout question are assumed to be unlikely to vote
  • Those who say "don't know" to the voting intention question are assumed to vote in the same way as GE 2024

Technical Details

Find Out Now polled 5,549 GB adults online between 4-11 September 2026. The sample was weighted to be representative by gender, age, social grade, other demographics and past voting patterns. Regression techniques were used to infer projected seat results.

Find Out Now and Electoral Calculus are both members of the British Polling Council and abide by its rules.

Regression Polling

Modern polling analysis often uses statistical regression techniques to get more accurate and geographically detailed results. Also called MRP (multi-level regression and post stratification) they have been used successfully by Electoral Calculus and other pollsters to predict general elections, local elections and the 2019 European elections.
