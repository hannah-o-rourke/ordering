MRP Poll October 2026
This page first posted 16 October 2026
On behalf of PLMR, Electoral Calculus has conducted a new MRP poll.
Polling using MRP, which stands for Multi-level Regression and Post-stratification, is a relatively recent innovation in polling science which have been used to successfully predict the last three British general elections,
and gives the estimated result in each Westminster constituency.
Fieldwork was conducted from 25 September - 2 October 2026, with a sample size of over 6,600 people.
More details at the 
PLMR website
.
1. Headlines
The poll places Labour on 28pc, ahead of Reform on 21pc and the Conservatives on 18pc.
On these figures, Labour is predicted to win 353 seats, giving it a comfortable majority of 56 seats.
The Conservatives are set to be the official opposition with 90 seats, with Reform as 
the third-largest party with 81 seats. 
The Liberal Democrats would win 70 seats, and the SNP 28 seats.
Plaid Cymru would win 5 seats, and the Greens 4.

3. Local Decisions
With devolution in the air, we asked our respondents "Who do you think should have the most say over decisions about local services, such as transport, housing and schools, in your area?"
Most people (52pc) said it should be their local council, with 25pc saying 'Don't know'. A further 12pc said it should be 'A regional mayor or combined authority',
and only 7pc said it should be the UK Government.

4. Treatment of "Don't Knows"
Following polling errors in 2024, Electoral Calculus took part in investigations into possible causes,
co-ordinated by the British Polling Council. Our findings are available
here
. In summary,
Centre-left voters were more likely to over-estimate their likelihood to turnout
Those who didn't know or refused to answer might have voted according to their GE 2019 preference
To incorporate these findings into our post-election polling, we have made two assumptions
about those who answer "don't know" to either the turnout or voting intention questions.
Those who say "don't know" to the turnout question are assumed to be unlikely to vote
Those who say "don't know" to the voting intention question are assumed to vote in the same way as GE 2024
Quotes
Kevin Craig
, founder of PLMR, who commissioned the poll said: "Our polling shows Labour on course for a majority, a clear sign that Andy Burnham’s leadership is resonating with voters. Much of that comes down to how he communicates. He speaks in plain English and explains what the Government is doing simply and directly. People know what he is offering and who it is for, and his digital campaign is cutting through the noise.
"There is still a long way to go before the next election, and this latest polling shows just how volatile British politics has become.
"Voters are clearly giving Labour another hearing, but they will want to see delivery on the issues that matter most to them, from the cost of living to public services, before they fully buy into the party again. The coming months will be crucial in determining whether Burnham can sustain this shift in support."
Martin Baxter
, founder of Electoral Calculus, said
"Our figures show a remarkable reversal of fortune for Labour under Andy Burnham, with the party now predicted to win 353 seats, an outright majority.
Burnham has used clear left-of-centre rhetoric to win back those voters who drifted to the Liberal Democrats and the Greens when Keir Starmer was leader, rather than chasing Reform voters.
"The right, by contrast, remains split. Restore Britain would win very few seats, but it is cutting into Reform's vote and leaving it with fewer than 100 seats and even less than the Conservatives.
A pact between the Conservatives and Reform would give them just 171 seats, not even half of Labour's 353. Under the first-past-the-post system the side that unites first wins and Andy Burnham clearly understands that and is acting on it."
Technical Details
Find Out Now polled 6,699 GB adults online between 25 September and 2 October June 2026. The sample was weighted to be representative by gender, age, social grade, other demographics and past voting patterns. Regression techniques were used to infer projected seat results.
Find Out Now and Electoral Calculus are both members of the British Polling Council and abide by its rules.

Regression Polling
Modern polling analysis often uses statistical regression techniques to get more accurate and geographically detailed results. Also called MRP (multi-level regression and post stratification) they have been used successfully by Electoral Calculus and other pollsters to predict general elections, local elections and the 2019 European elections.
