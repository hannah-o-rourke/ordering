## Q1. Party vote share by demographic (age, gender, class/NS-SEC, education, region, 2024 vote, Brexit vote, tenure, ethnicity/religion) — 2026 figures

### Takeaway
The only large-sample 2026 demographic release I could partly recover is YouGov's "How would Britain vote at the start of 2026?" (n>17,000, Dec 2025–Jan 2026 fieldwork): Reform 30% among men vs 22% women; peak 36% among people in their 60s; 37% among social renters vs 25% Green lead among private renters; only 38% of 2024 Labour voters retained. NatCen BSA 43 (published June 2026) gives the sharpest education gradient: Reform 40% among those below A-level vs 9% of graduates. By autumn 2026 the age gradient persists (18–24s: Greens/Labour lead; Reform 13.7% in a 7-poll average) but the Reform/Green levels are far lower than in spring.

## Q2. Why voters moved to Reform UK, how firm the support is, and the Reform coalition vs 2024 Con/Lab coalitions

### Takeaway
Every 2026 source agrees immigration is the dominant driver (YouGov: 61% of Labour→Reform switchers cite immigration still being too high; NatCen: 75% of Reform supporters say migrants undermine culture; 92% of switchers trust Reform over Conservatives on immigration), with cost of living a secondary driver that matters far more to ex-Labour (51%) than ex-Tory (30%) Reform voters. Firmness is asymmetric: ex-Conservative Reform voters are soft (73% willing to return to Tories) while ex-Labour Reform voters are hard (only 11% would consider Labour; 52% score Labour 0/10). Reform's share of the right bloc has fallen to ~50% with Restore Britain splitting off (40% of Reform voters "likely" to vote Restore per EC Sept poll).

### Cited Findings
- YouGov Big Voter Study 2026 ("How solid is Reform UK's rise?", mid-2026): immigration is "the top factor in Reform's rise" and supporters are "fairly uniform in that motivation"; 92% of switchers trust Reform more than the Conservatives on immigration; cost of living is a top concern for 51% of Reform's gains from Labour vs 30% of those from the Tories; 23–25% say Reform being "the only party to stand for people like them" or "closest to their values" is key; Reform's current support is "similar to the level that lost the Conservatives the last election" — [YouGov](https://yougov.com/en-gb/articles/55153-big-yougov-voter-study-2026-how-solid-is-reform-uks-rise)

## Q8. Progressive strategy research 2026: what messages work (Labour Together, Persuasion UK, Progressive Britain, Compass, Best for Britain, IPPR, Fabians, etc.)

### Takeaway
The dominant 2026 strategic conclusion from Persuasion UK, 38 Degrees, Fabians, IPPR and Compass is that Labour's recoverable voters are its *progressive* defectors (EU closeness, climate confidence, child poverty, public ownership, tax the rich) and that "squeeze"/lesser-evil or copy-Reform immigration messaging fails with Reform switchers (1–11% recoverable) while alienating the left; IPPR/Persuasion tests found fiscal rules without visible service improvement are punished and attack messages don't damage Labour's economic credibility. The counter-case (Luke Akehurst) that only Reform voters can restore a majority was contested by UCL and YouGov data. No 2026 Labour Together, Progressive Britain or Best for Britain report was found.
