## Q1. Scotland: 2026 Holyrood polling, the 7 May 2026 result, government formation, post-election VI and independence polling

### Takeaway
The SNP won the 7 May 2026 Scottish Parliament election with 58 of 129 seats (−6), short of the 65 needed for a majority; Reform UK (17, from zero) and Labour (17, −5, its lowest ever) tied for second, the Greens took 15, the Conservatives 12 (their lowest ever) and the Lib Dems 10 (search summary of Wikipedia/SPICe). John Swinney was re-elected First Minister on 19 May 2026 in a three-round contested vote (final round 56 of 105 votes) with no formal deal announced in the chamber, and formed an SNP-only cabinet on 21 May 2026 (official report). Pre-election poll-by-poll figures could not be retrieved (see Gaps).

### Inferences
- The 58 + 15 = 73 pro-independence MSPs is indeed a record (2021: SNP 64 + Green 8 = 72), consistent with Swinney's "largest-ever" claim.
- Swinney won in round 3 with 56 of 105 only because opposition MSPs stopped voting after their candidates were eliminated (128 → 115 → 105 votes cast); Labour, Reform and Green MSPs kept voting for their own nominees to the end, so no party publicly backed the SNP. Combined with the all-SNP cabinet, this points to an SNP minority government without a formal Green agreement, but no source read here states the arrangement explicitly.
- The appointment of a "newly elected MSP for Shetland" as an SNP minister implies the SNP gained Shetland (held by the Lib Dems since 1999) — unverified from results data.
- Offord's "2 million did not vote / 47 per cent" implies turnout around 53%, below 2021's 63.5% — treat as approximate until checked against SPICe.
- Flynn and Gethins resigning from the Commons on 14 May implies both won Holyrood seats on 7 May (SNP rules bar dual mandates) — consistent with their Scottish Government appointments.

## Q2. Wales: 2026 Senedd polling, the 7 May 2026 result under the 96-seat closed-list system, First Minister, post-election polling

### Takeaway
Plaid Cymru won the first election under the new 16-constituency, 96-seat closed-list system with 43 seats on 35.4%, six short of the 49 needed for a majority; Reform UK took 34 seats (29.3%) to become the official opposition, Labour collapsed to 9 seats (its first time not the largest party, with First Minister Eluned Morgan losing her seat), the Conservatives won 7, Greens 2 and Lib Dems 1 (search summaries). Rhun ap Iorwerth was nominated First Minister on 12 May 2026 by 44 votes to Dan Thomas (Reform) 34 and Darren Millar (Con) 7, with 9 abstentions (official Senedd Record). Pre-election polls could not be retrieved.

### Cited Findings
Result (search summaries; pages not fetchable):
- Plaid Cymru 43 seats, 35.4% — "its best result since devolution began", six short of the 49-seat majority; Reform UK 34 seats, 29.3%, "its first seats in the Senedd"; Labour 9; Welsh Conservatives 7; Greens 2; Liberal Democrats 1 — [Institute for Government explainer](https://www.instituteforgovernment.org.uk/explainer/senedd-cymru-welsh-parliament); [Wikipedia: 2026 Senedd election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Senedd_election); [Swansea Bay News](https://swanseabaynews.com/senedd-election-2026-plaid-cymru-largest-party-reform-uk-historic-breakthrough-welsh-labour-reduced-to-nine-seats-the-new-political-map-of-wales/) (an early Swansea Bay count gave Plaid 41 before the final 43).
- Outgoing First Minister Eluned Morgan lost her own seat — reported as the first time a sitting leader of a UK government lost their seat — and Labour ceased to be the largest party in Wales for the first time — [Wikipedia: 2026 Senedd election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Senedd_election) (search summary).
- Wikipedia lists Rhun ap Iorwerth as First Minister from 12 May 2026 and has an "Ap Iorwerth government" article — [Wikipedia: Ap Iorwerth government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ap_Iorwerth_government); [Wikipedia: Rhun ap Iorwerth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhun_ap_Iorwerth).

First Minister nomination (primary, verified from the Senedd Record):
- 12 May 2026, item 3 "Nomination of the First Minister under Standing Order 8", presided over by the new Llywydd Huw Irranca-Davies (Labour). Nominations: Rhun ap Iorwerth (nominated by Zaynub Akbar, Plaid), Dan Thomas (nominated by Llŷr Powell, Reform UK), Darren Millar (nominated by Andrew RT Davies, Conservative). Roll-call result: "Rhun ap Iorwerth 44, Dan Thomas 34, Darren Millar seven, abstentions nine. The total number of votes is 94. Saved as `data/senedd_first_minister_nomination_2026-05-12_official_record.csv`. Note: the WebSearch summary of Wikipedia said "45 votes drawn from Plaid MSs and the two Green MSs"; the official Record says 44 — prefer 44.
- Ap Iorwerth's acceptance speech congratulated "party leaders: Jane Dodds, Darren Millar and Ken Skates on their re-election", applauded the Green Party's "historic breakthrough", and congratulated "Dan Thomas and the Reform UK group on securing what will no doubt prove to be a formidable presence"; he referred to "the Plaid Cymru Government" — same source.

### Inferences
- The roll call (44 + 34 + 7 + 9 = 94 of 96 MSs) is consistent with Plaid 43 + Greens 2 = 45 minus one absentee or the Llywydd not voting, Reform 34 all for Thomas, Conservatives 7 for Millar, and Labour (8 excluding the Llywydd) plus the single Lib Dem abstaining. So Plaid governs as a minority with no formal partner recorded in the nomination debate.
- Ap Iorwerth's reference to "Ken Skates" among party leaders "re-elected" strongly implies Ken Skates had become Welsh Labour leader/Senedd group leader after Eluned Morgan lost her seat — not independently verified.
- Darren Millar remained Welsh Conservative leader; Jane Dodds remained Welsh Lib Dem leader (sole LD MS); Dan Thomas is Reform UK's Senedd group leader and Leader of the Opposition.
